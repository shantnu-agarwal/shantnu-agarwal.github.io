# 3. About page

H1:
A little more about me.

Supporting line:
An engineer by education, a product manager by experience, and a curious person by nature.

Opening:
I'm Shantnu. I enjoy figuring out how things work, understanding why they work the way they do, and exploring ideas that catch my attention.

My interests have taken me in quite a few directions—from software engineering and product management to business strategy, photography, music, and technology.

Some of those interests became part of my career. Others have remained things I enjoy simply for the sake of it.

Here's a little about how I got here.

## Where it all started

I was born in Roorkee, Uttarakhand, and grew up in Noida, where my family moved when I was around six.

I studied Computer Science and Engineering at SRM Institute of Science and Technology in Chennai, graduating in 2021.

College was an opportunity to explore technology beyond the classroom. I became involved with the Microsoft Student Partner community and eventually led an 80-member campus team, organising events and working with students who shared similar interests.

I developed a mental-health self-assessment app for SRM Medical Hospital on a pro-bono basis and explored voice-controlled web interfaces through academic research. That work resulted in two research publications in 2021 and 2022.

What I particularly enjoyed was having an idea, understanding how it might work, and seeing whether I could build it.

## From writing code to building products

I started my professional career at Bajaj Finserv Health in June 2021 as a software developer.

One of my early assignments involved taking over an incomplete sales application. With a small engineering team and direct access to the people using the product, I found myself doing more than writing code.

I was speaking with business and sales teams, understanding their problems, deciding how to address them, and then building the solutions.

It was an early lesson in the difference between delivering software and solving a problem.

In June 2022, I transitioned into Technical Product Management.

Over the next few years, I worked on customer-facing digital experiences, enterprise integrations, insurance technology and large-scale software platforms.

I had the opportunity to work with people across engineering, design, operations, business and leadership teams.

An engineer might focus on feasibility. Someone in operations might see the exceptions that happen every day. A business leader might be thinking about cost, growth or risk.

The interesting challenge is finding a solution that accounts for those perspectives.

By July 2025, I had moved into a Senior Technical Product Manager role, working increasingly on enterprise solutions, business priorities and initiatives that crossed organisational boundaries.

I left Bajaj Finserv Health in March 2026, after nearly five years.

My time there shaped how I approach problems and gave me a deeper appreciation for what it takes to build technology that works in the real world.

## Looking at the bigger picture

In April 2026, I joined the one-year MBA programme (PGPX) at the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad.

After spending most of my career building technology and products, I wanted to understand more about the businesses around them.

Why do some businesses succeed while others struggle? How do organisations make decisions when there isn't an obvious right answer? What makes a strategy work in practice?

Business school has given me an opportunity to explore those questions through economics, finance, marketing, strategy and conversations with people from a wide range of industries.

I'm also involved with IIMA's Product Management Club as its Placement Cell Head, helping fellow students prepare for product-management opportunities.

For me, the MBA isn't about leaving technology behind. It's about adding another dimension to how I understand and approach problems.

## The things that keep me curious

My interests don't always have much in common.

I've always enjoyed technology, particularly understanding what's happening underneath the surface. Whether it's a new gadget, an AI model or an unfamiliar system, I like going beyond what something does to understand how it works.

Photography and videography are another part of my life. I enjoy exploring new places, noticing details and capturing moments that I might otherwise forget.

I also fly a DJI drone recreationally. It's an enjoyable way to explore a different visual perspective.

Music has been a longstanding interest, especially percussion instruments such as the tabla and drums.

I enjoy playing badminton and table tennis, too.

And I love travelling. Being somewhere unfamiliar and experiencing a different culture is something I genuinely enjoy.

Not everything I do needs to contribute to a career goal or lead somewhere productive. Sometimes, curiosity is reason enough.

## A few things I've learned along the way

This section MUST appear near the bottom.

### Stay curious.

Some of the most useful things I've learned began with a simple question. I've found that asking why—and being willing to question what I think I know—often leads somewhere interesting.

### Understand the details.

There's a difference between knowing that something works and understanding how it works. I enjoy getting into the details, connecting the pieces, and building an understanding that goes beyond the surface.

### Make room for different perspectives.

Working with people from different backgrounds has taught me that my first interpretation of a problem isn't necessarily the most useful one.

Some of the best solutions emerge when people challenge one another's assumptions and bring different experiences to the conversation.

These aren't rules I always follow perfectly. They're ideas I try to keep coming back to.

## Still exploring

I'm not entirely sure where all my interests will take me, and I'm comfortable with that.

I'd like to keep building useful things, understanding complex problems, learning from interesting people and exploring whatever catches my curiosity.

If any of that resonates with you, I'd be happy to connect.

### Challenge

Large-scale insurance processing involves policy conditions, medical assessments, financial rules, documentation and numerous exceptions.

Many workflows historically required manual intervention.

The challenge was translating complex insurance operations into systematic, scalable software workflows.

### Role and approach

Led end-to-end scoping, planning, solutioning, development, testing and delivery of a government-sector enterprise SaaS platform.

Worked across more than 150 internal colleagues and stakeholders and participated in external demonstrations, walkthroughs and training.

Helped move manual claims-verification and adjudication steps into system-driven workflows.

- Coverage limits for medical conditions
- Bill itemisation
- Medical case assignment
- Claims-processing rules

### Outcomes

- Programme covering approximately 7 million lives
- Approximately 2 million insurance claims in scope
- First software revenue for the government business division

Do not call these 7 million active users.
Do not claim 2 million monthly transactions.
Do not name the state government.

### Reflection

The most interesting challenge was translating complex operational processes into technology while aligning people with very different responsibilities.

## Featured case study 3

Slug:
insurance-rules-automation

Title:
Finding the business problem behind the technical problem.

Category:
Business Transformation

Role:
Senior Technical Product Manager

Period:
2025–2026

### Challenge

Incorrect or inconsistent application of insurance rules created revenue leakage and required manual payment adjustments by operations teams.

Relevant rules included copayments and condition-specific coverage caps.

### Role and approach

Worked with business, operations and technology teams to identify the underlying rules problem and implement an automated solution.

The affected rules were relevant to approximately 80% of maternity claims processed daily.

### Outcome

Implemented automated rule application to address revenue leakage and reduce dependence on manual adjustments.

No validated monetary savings, recovery amount or precise reduction in manual effort has been supplied.

DO NOT invent those figures.

### Reflection

The most valuable business problems often sit between organisational functions.

Solving them requires understanding how business, operations and technology perspectives fit together.
